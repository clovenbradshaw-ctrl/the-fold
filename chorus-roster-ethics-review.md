# The chorus roster, and where women and non-binary scholars belong in it

Started as a proposal (VI.2 — propose without disposing); the user then
directed "rename as needed," so the recommendations below marked **▶
applied** are now live in `verification.js`/`CHORUS-LOG.md`/CLAUDE.md's
own pointer convention (POLICIES.md P93 is the law for the decision;
this file remains the law for the research behind it). This document
does two things the user asked for directly: checks that the people this
repo has named review-personas and "givers" after actually hold up
against the project's own values (honesty, rigor, non-fabrication,
giving credit, epistemic humility), and names specific women and
non-binary scholars who have earned a place in this roster, with the
real work backing each name.

Every claim below was checked against live sources this pass, not recalled
from memory alone — the same discipline this repo asks of everything else
it cites. Sources are linked per person. Where a source disagreed with an
earlier draft's claim, the claim was corrected or dropped rather than kept.

## Where the naming actually happens

Two different practices, worth keeping apart:

1. **The chorus roster** (`CHORUS-LOG.md`, `verification.js`'s `PERSONAS`)
   — eleven real (mostly) people stand in for eleven review disciplines,
   used both for adversarial code-review passes and as labels on the
   nine-cell verification grid. This is the closest thing in the repo to
   literally "naming an organ after a person."
2. **"Giver" citations** — the repo's standing rule that a received idea,
   statistic, or closed word-class "enters with its giver named or it
   does not enter" (`priors.js`'s own discipline, restated throughout
   CLAUDE.md). This is ordinary academic attribution, not persona-naming,
   but the user's ask covers it too, and it turned out to matter — see
   Fisher and Russell below.

### The two real flags

**Feynman (DEF).** His scientific integrity is uncontested — nobody
disputes the physics or the "you are the easiest person to fool" ethic
this repo cites him for. But his own memoir, *Surely You're Joking, Mr.
Feynman!*, contains self-narrated chapters describing transactional,
objectifying pickup tactics toward women, which have drawn sustained
recent criticism (Scientific American; *The Baffler*) as normalizing
sexist conduct rather than harmless bravado. Separately and more
seriously: his second wife's 1956 divorce filing — reported at the time
by the LA Times and referenced in his later-released FBI file — alleged
violent rages and physical altercations. This was never criminally
charged or adjudicated, so it is credible-but-unproven, not settled fact.
Neither item touches the physics; both are worth the user knowing before
endorsing the name.
[Slate](https://slate.com/technology/2019/01/richard-feynman-physical-abuse-science-wife-fbi.html) ·
[Scientific American](https://blogs.scientificamerican.com/the-curious-wavefunction/richard-feynman-sexism-and-changing-perceptions-of-a-scientific-icon/) ·
[The Baffler](https://thebaffler.com/outbursts/surely-youre-a-creep-mr-feynman-mcneill)

**Marshall (meta).** Nothing in the repo names a full person for
"Marshall" — this is an inference, not a citation. The role (checking
that the *other* reviewers' findings actually conform to the project's
own founding document) is close to a textbook description of what John
Marshall's *Marbury v. Madison* (1803) invented: judicial review, the
power to check an act against the constitution. That is a strong
thematic fit. It is also the same John Marshall who was a lifelong
Virginia slaveholder, and who authored *Johnson v. M'Intosh* (1823),
which imported the European "Doctrine of Discovery" into U.S. law and
supplied the legal basis for centuries of Native land dispossession —
still cited today in modern repudiations of that doctrine (by churches
and, in 2023, a formal Vatican renunciation). If "Marshall" is meant to
be John Marshall, this is a real, unresolved tension between an excellent
conceptual fit and a genuinely serious historical record, and it deserves
a decision, not a default.
[Doctrine of Discovery, Johnson v. M'Intosh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doctrine_of_discovery) ·
[John Marshall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Marshall)

*(The alternative reading, Thurgood Marshall — first Black Justice, lead
counsel in Brown v. Board of Education, decades of civil-rights
litigation — is an unimpeachable figure, but his signature contribution
is civil-rights advocacy, not founding-document conformance-checking as a
doctrine. He used judicial review; John Marshall invented it. If the name
is ever confirmed as intentionally Thurgood Marshall, there is no flag at
all.)*

### One disambiguation that was settled, even though it turned out low-risk

**Holmes (SIG) — resolved by replacement, not by picking a reading.**
Never given a full name in the repo. Three
candidates were checked: Sherlock Holmes (fictional detective — "you see,
but you do not observe," the closest real match to "identity/
surface-overlap" deduction); Oliver Wendell Holmes Jr. (Justice, but
author of *Buck v. Bell* (1927), "three generations of imbeciles are
enough" — one of the most disgraced opinions in Supreme Court history,
later cited by Nazi Germany's own lawyers defending forced sterilization,
never formally overruled); and Oliver Wendell Holmes Sr. (physician, weak
fit either way). Given that Chekhov sits on the same roster named for a
*technique* ("Chekhov's gun") rather than a discipline, and that neither
real Holmes is specifically known for identity-deduction work, the
fictional detective is the better-supported reading. Low risk either way,
but worth stating plainly which one this is, since the wrong reading
carried the worst single flag of anyone considered in this review — which
is exactly why the seat now names Barbara Grosz instead of resolving the
ambiguity in favor of either Holmes (Part 3, item 2).
[Buck v. Bell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buck_v._Bell)

### Everyone else: clean

Diaconis, Dijkstra, Simon, Frankfurt, Ostrom, Pearl, Alexander, and
Chekhov all returned "no significant ethical concerns found" against a
genuine search (not just an absence of memory) — the closest thing to
individual controversy on record for any of them is Dijkstra's blunt
professional manner and Alexander's later work being contested as
unscientific within architecture theory, neither of which is an ethics
finding. Full source lists are in the research transcripts this document

## Part 2 — the "giver" citations, checked

Sixteen scholars/traditions cited as the origin of a concept, statistic,
or closed word-class this repo implements. Fourteen returned clean or
"not applicable" (the three ancient grammarians — Pāṇini, Sibawayh,
Dionysius Thrax — have no reliable modern-style biographical record to
check at all). Two carry real, documented concerns, separable from the
specific thing being cited but worth disclosing exactly the way this
project already discloses everything else it measures:

**Bertrand Russell** (cited for the Theory of Descriptions / presupposition
logic, alongside Strawson). His logical work carries no taint. Separately,
he was a lifelong, documented advocate of eugenics — most explicitly in
*Marriage and Morals* (1929), arguing for state sterilization of people he
called "mentally defective." His biographer Ray Monk treats this as a real
strand of his thought, not a caricature, though Russell grew more
skeptical of genetic determinism later in life.
[Bertrand Russell on Eugenics](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/283125075_Bertrand_Russell_on_Eugenics)

**Ronald Fisher** (cited narrowly for Fisher's exact test — the P-value
math behind, e.g., the "Fisher exact p = 0.0180" figure in this repo's
`kind-standing.js`/`fold-gate.js` work, and its sibling citation in
eoreader7's bridge-witness measurement). This is the most serious flag in
either part of this review. Fisher was not a one-off or a product of his
era in the passive sense: he co-founded the Cambridge University Eugenics
Society as an undergraduate, held the Galton Professorship of Eugenics at
UCL for two decades, edited the *Annals of Eugenics*, and campaigned for
pronatalist policy favoring the "professional classes." In 1950 he was
one of a small handful of scientists to formally, publicly dissent from
UNESCO's Statement on Race, writing that science supported the claim that
"the groups of mankind differ in their innate capacity for intellectual
and emotional development" — an explicit rejection of the emerging
anti-racist scientific consensus, not silence or ambiguity. Separately, as
a paid consultant to the tobacco industry late in life, he argued the
smoking–cancer link was better explained by genetic confounding — a
position judged then and since to have been compromised by that funding.
Cambridge's Gonville & Caius College and UCL have since removed his
commemorative honors.
[New Statesman](https://www.newstatesman.com/long-reads/2020/07/ra-fisher-and-science-hatred) ·
[Prosocial World](https://www.prosocial.world/posts/ronald-fisher-is-not-being-cancelled-but-his-eugenic-advocacy-should-have-consequences) ·
[Priceonomics, tobacco](https://priceonomics.com/why-the-father-of-modern-statistics-didnt-believe/)

Neither finding makes the *mathematics* wrong — the exact test is valid,
established, indispensable, and this review is not proposing this repo
stop using it. What it proposes is Part 3's fourth item: name the origin
story fully rather than only the one name that survived it.

## Part 3 — where women and non-binary scholars belong

Four concrete placements, each backed by real, checked work — not a
quota exercise. One open seat, one weak seat, one contested seat, and two
citation corrections.

### 1. ▶ Applied — Atmosphere / REC, filled with **Barbara McClintock**

`verification.js`'s own comment says this seat has no confirmed occupant:
*"Simon is carried here as a disclosed SUGGESTION, not a confirmed
reuse."* REC's job is recognizing when evidence has broken a ground past
tolerance, formally conceding it, and admitting a genuinely new one — not
incremental revision, a re-zero.

Barbara McClintock discovered transposable elements ("jumping genes") in
maize in the 1940s–50s — genetic material that moves and reorganizes the
genome, which is close to a literal biological re-zero. The finding was
dismissed and largely ignored by the genetics establishment for roughly
three decades before being vindicated; she was elected to the National
Academy of Sciences (1944, one of the first women), and won an *unshared*
1983 Nobel Prize in Physiology or Medicine — still the only woman to win
that category's prize alone. No documented ethical concerns. This is
arguably a sharper, cleaner match for REC than any of the men currently
holding a confirmed cell.
[McClintock, Nobel](https://www.nobelprize.org/prizes/medicine/1983/mcclintock/facts/)

*A second candidate was researched and is disclosed rather than quietly
dropped: **Lynn Margulis**, whose serial endosymbiotic theory (mitochondria
and chloroplasts as absorbed free-living bacteria) is an equally strong
REC story — roughly 15 rejections before her 1967 paper, later National
Academy of Sciences election and National Medal of Science. She is not
this review's recommendation, because later in life she was a public
HIV/AIDS denialist and 9/11-conspiracy endorser — positions that refuse
to concede ground to overwhelming evidence, which is a real irony for a
persona whose whole job is doing exactly that. Named here so the choice
between the two is the user's, made with the full picture, not made by
omission.*
[Margulis, NSF](https://www.nsf.gov/honorary-awards/national-medal-science/recipients/lynn-margulis) ·
[on the HIV/9-11 positions](https://gizmodo.com/carl-sagans-son-is-a-9-11-truther-1690715187)

### 2. ▶ Applied — SIG / Entity, Holmes replaced with **Barbara Grosz**

Holmes checked out as low-risk fiction (Part 1) — there was no ethical
obligation to replace it. It was replaced anyway, because a real scholar
whose actual research answers "does this surface form refer to the same
entity as an earlier one" — which is literally what the SIG/Entity cell
checks — is a better match on the merits than a fictional analogy, not
only a safer one. She
co-developed Centering Theory (with Aravind Joshi and Scott Weinstein),
the foundational framework for exactly this question in computational
linguistics: tracking which discourse entity is in focus so pronouns and
repeated names resolve correctly. She holds the Higgins Professorship at
Harvard SEAS, was AAAI's first woman president, Radcliffe Institute's
first dean of science, and an ACM/AAAI/AAAS Fellow. No ethical concerns
found.
[Grosz, Harvard SEAS](https://seas.harvard.edu/news/barbara-j-grosz-wins-acmaaai-allen-newell-award) ·
[Centering Theory (1995)](https://aclanthology.org/J95-2003/)

### 3. ▶ Applied — meta, Marshall replaced with **Helen Longino**

This is the sharpest conceptual match found in the whole review, stronger
even than the Marbury v. Madison parallel. Longino's research program —
"contextual empiricism" — argues that scientific objectivity is not
produced by individual genius or detachment but by a *community's*
transparent, criticizable standards: real venues for criticism, actual
uptake of that criticism, publicly shared norms, roughly equal
intellectual authority among participants. That is a near-exact
description of what CHORUS-LOG.md *is* as a practice, and of what the
meta persona is supposed to audit — whether the reviewers are actually
holding each other to the shared written standard, not just performing
review. Author of *Science as Social Knowledge* (1990) and *The Fate of
Knowledge* (2002), Professor Emerita of Philosophy at Stanford. No
ethical concerns found.
[Longino, Stanford](https://cap.stanford.edu/profiles/frdActionServlet?choiceId=printerprofile&profileversion=full&profileId=52786)

*If the project prefers to stay inside the judicial register rather than
move to philosophy of science, **Constance Baker Motley** is the cleaner
in-register alternative: NAACP Legal Defense Fund lawyer (mentored by
Thurgood Marshall), first Black woman appointed a U.S. federal judge,
principal author of the original Brown v. Board of Education complaint,
argued ten cases before the Supreme Court and won nine. Meticulous
constitutional draftsmanship is her actual signature, which is close to
what "meta" checks for.*

### 4. ▶ Recorded — two citation corrections, no removals

(Checked: neither Fisher nor Halliday is cited by name in this repo's own
code — both citations live in CLAUDE.md prose. There was no code-level
"giver:" line to edit, so the correction below is recorded in POLICIES.md
P93 and here, the same register the original citations were made in,
rather than invented as a code comment with nothing to attach to.)

- **Muriel Bristol**, wherever Fisher's exact test is cited (this repo's
  `kind-standing.js`/`fold-gate.js`, and eoreader7's bridge-witness work).
  She was a real, credentialed scientist — a PhD algologist working at
  Rothamsted Experimental Station — whose claim that she could taste
  whether milk or tea was poured first is the actual origin of the test:
  Fisher designed the randomized eight-cup experiment specifically to
  evaluate her claim, and by the more-cited accounts she passed it. The
  test is usually taught with only Fisher's name attached to an
  experiment that exists because of her claim. Naming her alongside him
  is exactly what this project's own "a giver enters named or does not
  enter" rule already asks for — this is that rule applied to itself.
  [Muriel Bristol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muriel_Bristol) ·
  [Lady tasting tea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lady_tasting_tea)

- **Ruqaiya Hasan**, wherever Systemic Functional Grammar / cohesion
  concepts are cited (currently Halliday alone, in the grammar-lens
  section of CLAUDE.md). *Cohesion in English* (1976) is a full,
  equal-billing co-authored work, not a footnoted assist, and her
  independent work on semantic variation and register shaped SFL's later
  trajectory (her Macquarie University obituary credits her with
  "directing the trajectory" of the field). The honest version of this
  story: she began as Halliday's student and research assistant and
  became a genuinely independent, prominent co-originator — that
  nuance is worth keeping rather than flattening into either "pure
  erasure" or "junior assistant."
  [Hasan, Macquarie obituary](https://www.mq.edu.au/about_us/faculties_and_departments/faculty_of_human_sciences/linguistics/linguistics_news_events/vale_emeritus_professor_ruqaiya_hasan/)

*A third possible addition, not urgent enough to force in: **Anna
Wierzbicka**, whose Natural Semantic Metalanguage program (empirically
derived cross-linguistic semantic primitives, built specifically to avoid
assuming one language's grammar is universal) speaks directly to the
concern CLAUDE.md's own grammar-lens section raises about "verb" being
borrowed from Greek grammar "without a receipt." She'd be a citation
alongside Pāṇini/Sibawayh/Thrax, not a replacement for any of them.*

### On non-binary representation, stated honestly

Only one candidate researched this pass has non-binary identity that is
clearly, publicly self-documented rather than retroactively assigned:
**Sasha Costanza-Chock** (non-binary, trans femme, she/they), author of
*Design Justice: Community-Led Practices to Build the Worlds We Need*
(MIT Press, 2020; 2021 PROSE Award), whose core thesis — naming who
actually holds the power to design a system, and for whom — pairs well
with Ostrom's commons-governance work (CON) or Alexander's design-pattern
legacy (SYN), as a complement rather than a replacement for either, since
neither currently has a flag. This is not a large list, and that is worth
saying plainly rather than padding it: most of the traditions this repo
cites are decades-to-millennia old, and treating "non-binary" as a
category historical figures either were or weren't is its own kind of
anachronism. The honest fix is to keep looking toward living scholars as
the work continues, not to force a single pass to solve it.
[Costanza-Chock, Design Justice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sasha_Costanza-Chock)

## What was applied, and what was deliberately left alone

**Applied** (POLICIES.md P93 is the law; `verification.js`'s `PERSONAS`
and `CHORUS-LOG.md`'s new roster note are the code): Entity/SIG → Barbara
Grosz; Atmosphere/REC → Barbara McClintock (a seat that was never
confirmed, not one that was replaced); meta → Helen Longino. The
Bristol/Hasan citation corrections are recorded in POLICIES.md P93 and
this file, since neither Fisher nor Halliday had a code-level citation
line to edit.

**Left alone, on purpose, not by default:** every historical entry in
`CHORUS-LOG.md` — the log is append-only, and a past review's recorded
reviewer name is a fact about what happened, not a live label; rewriting
it would be exactly the kind of quiet history-edit this repo refuses
everywhere else. Feynman keeps his seat — the concern is real and now
disclosed in three places (here, `CHORUS-LOG.md`'s roster note, POLICIES
P93), but no replacement was researched, and swapping him for an
unresearched name would repeat the failure this whole review exists to
correct. Russell's and Fisher's underlying citations (the logic, the
exact test) stand — the documented concern in each case is biographical,
not mathematical, and no alternative was found carrying the same
technical content. None of this is quietly declared "solved"; the
non-binary seat named in the section above is a name kept in mind, not a
seat filled, and is disclosed as exactly that rather than padded out.
