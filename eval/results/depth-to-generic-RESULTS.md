# Depth-to-generic on labelled restatements — a negative result, chased to its cause

2026-09-08. `node eval/depth-to-generic.mjs`. Answers the gap named against an
earlier, unwritten-up result (reported informally: L0/L1/L2/L3 hand-built
probes went generic at hop 7/5/3/3, monotone) — does the same separation
hold for restatements that are *labelled by declaration*, not hand-picked to
land where the detector says?

**Setup.** `relative.js::Field` over `eoreader7/native/eval/the-fold/
fixtures/tolstoy-borodino.txt` (732 passages). 24 restatements, 6 per level,
each generated by a real `gemma2:2b` call that *declares* the level in its
own prompt (event / person / relation / theme) and is never inspected before
labelling — the label is what was asked for, not what came back. Each is
about a different real seed passage spread across the corpus, never four
versions of one topic. `relative.js::nullBand`/`recallAgainstNull` gained
additive `steps`/`spread` options the same day (backward-compatible, tested)
so a hop-N recall can be checked against a hop-N-measured null instead of
the field's own default of hop 1 — needed for this measurement to be sound
at all.

## Result: no separation

24/24 restatements go generic at hop 1, at every declared level. Mean
depth-to-generic is 1.00 for all four levels — a tie, not an ordering. The
"monotone (ties allowed)" check reports `true` only because a tie is
trivially non-decreasing; the shuffled-label null (II.23 — a control run
before the result is trusted) confirms this is vacuous: shuffling which
restatement carries which label reproduces the identical "monotone" reading
in 200/200 draws, because with all four means equal, every one of the 4! label
permutations passes the same trivial check. **This is not the 7/5/3/3
finding reproduced on independent data. It is a floor effect that must be
diagnosed before either result can be trusted.**

## Diagnosis, measured rather than assumed

**Not a vocabulary problem.** Word-level overlap between each restatement
and the corpus's own vocabulary is 0.71–0.92 for every full-sentence item
(only the one-word item, "Loneliness.", is nearer zero). The model is not
paraphrasing into alien words.

**Activation clears the null band's `hi` in nearly every case** — often 1.5–2×
`band.hi`. The mechanism is not blind to these cues. What fails is the
**margin** check: the field settles on several passages at close to equal
strength, so even a correct top match reads `ambiguous`. Two of the 24 items
land their top match on their own exact seed passage and are *still* called
ambiguous, because the runner-up is too close (`lead` 0.000–0.060, smaller
than the null's own best margin in every row).

**Not a corpus-size effect.** The identical 24 restatements were re-run,
unchanged, against a field built from the whole book (`pg2600.txt`, 10,327
passages — 14× the excerpt) rather than the 732-passage excerpt. Same
result: 0/24 figure hits, mean margin 0.055–0.082 across all four levels,
no separation. Going from 732 to 10,327 nodes changed nothing.

**The pipeline itself works — a positive control.** Six literal fragments
of real seed passages (30% of the words, taken verbatim — the exact cue
shape the spec's own `eval/relative-addresses.mjs` measured at 119–120/120)
were run through the identical field, identical code path: 6/6 land
`figure`, correctly, on their own exact source passage, at 2–3× the null
band's `hi`. The measurement is sound; the null result on paraphrases is
real.

## What this means, stated at the scope it's earned

This SDR (word + ordered-bigram hash) representation concentrates activation
cleanly on cues that echo a passage's own literal phrasing — which is what
every task in the original spec's own evidence table (§0) actually used:
whole passages, fragments, corrupted copies, shifted and renamed sources —
never a free paraphrase in different words. A genuine natural-language
restatement, even with 70–90% vocabulary overlap with the corpus, does not
reproduce a passage's specific bigram structure closely enough to win the
margin check, on this corpus, at either scale tested. **This is a finding
about the mechanism, not a bug in this driver.**

This does not disprove the reported 7/5/3/3 result — that measurement is not
reproduced here, but it was never run here either, and the two experiments
may have used different corpora, different fields, or (most likely, given
the margin-not-activation failure mode found here) probes that were closer
paraphrases of source phrasing than the fully independent ones generated for
this run. What this run establishes is narrower and, on its own terms,
solid: **for genuinely independent restatements in a model's own words, the
current mechanism does not distinguish abstraction level — it does not
resolve cleanly at all, regardless of level, on two real corpora three
orders of magnitude apart in size.** Reading the level off an arbitrary
restatement's hop-to-generic is not established, and the reason it fails is
now known rather than guessed at.

**Named, not attempted here:** whether a restatement closer to source
phrasing (a guided paraphrase, or a phrase-level rather than sentence-level
cue) restores the margin; whether a smaller `spread` or a representation
sensitive to distinctiveness (down-weighting bits common to many nodes —
closer to tf-idf than raw hashing) would let a genuine paraphrase win the
margin check the way a fragment already does. Both are real, falsifiable
next steps, not run.
